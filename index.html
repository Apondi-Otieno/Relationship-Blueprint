This is not about one relationship. It is a complete picture of how safe you currently are inside a relationship — your wounds, your patterns, your triggers, and your navigation plan. Answer every section honestly. The blueprint analyses your answers and tells you whether Phase 1 is ready to open.

A — Closing the last chapter

Before a new phase begins, the last one must close. These questions are about the most recent significant relationship — or the one that still lives rent-free in your head.

1
Your honest 50%
What was your part in how it ended? Not his failures — yours specifically. What did you do, avoid, permit, or ignore that you already knew?
♡ Freedom meditation

Sit quietly, one hand on your heart. Take three slow breaths.

Breathe in the thought: “Something happened, and I played a part. I am not a victim and I am not a villain. I am someone who is learning to see clearly.”

On each exhale, release one specific thing you did that you wish you had done differently. Not with shame — with honesty. There is a difference.

Repeat for 5 minutes. Each exhale: one thing you owned. Each inhale: one breath of your own freedom to choose differently next time.

2
What you are still waiting for
What are you still waiting for him to give you? An apology, closure, an explanation, proof that you mattered? Name it — then name how you could begin giving it to yourself.
♡ Cord-cutting meditation

Close your eyes. Breathe slowly. Imagine an energetic cord connecting your belly to theirs.

On each out-breath, gently dissolve a little more of it. Say inwardly: “I release you to your path. I reclaim my energy for my own life.”

Repeat 3 times. One minute of normal breathing between each. Notice what rises — resistance, grief, or relief. All of it is useful data.

3
The gift
What did this relationship genuinely give you — even if it hurt? A lesson, a clarity, a red flag you will never ignore again, a standard you now hold.
♡ Loving-kindness meditation

Sit quietly. Think of the smallest genuinely kind thing he did — even something tiny. Hold it without minimising it and without inflating it.

Breathe in: “May you be happy. May you be free from suffering.”

If anger rises, go smaller: “May you find a parking space.” This is not for him. It is to unclench your heart so you can receive the gift without the resentment blocking it.

B — Your wounds

A wound is not a flaw. It is a place that was hurt before you had the tools to protect it. Every person carries them. The question is whether yours are named — because unnamed wounds make all your relationship decisions on your behalf.

4
Name the wound
Which of these do you recognise in yourself? Choose what fits and write about it:

Fear of abandonment I am not enough I must earn love Fear of being controlled Fear of intimacy I am invisible I am too much I self-abandon to keep peace I do not trust my own judgment I am only valuable if I am useful
♡ Naming meditation

Sit still. Place one hand on your chest. Take three slow breaths.

Say the wound you named out loud — even quietly, even in a whisper. Notice what your body does when you say it. Tightening, softness, recognition, shame. All of it is information.

Then say: “I see this in me. It does not define me. And I am no longer going to let it make my decisions.”

Breathe. Stay with that for 2 minutes.

5
Where it came from
When did you first feel this? Was it a parent, a sibling, an early experience of loss or humiliation or neglect? You do not need to over-explain — just locate it.
♡ Inner child meditation

Imagine your wise, current self meeting your inner child at the age you first felt this wound. Do not fix or explain. Simply hold her hand and say: “I see you. You are safe now. I will never leave you.” Stay 5–10 minutes. If tears come, let them.

6
How it behaves inside a relationship
When this wound is active, what does it make you do? Be specific — not emotional, behavioural. What do you tolerate, chase, avoid, perform, shrink from, or become?
♡ Body scan meditation

Sit comfortably. Close your eyes. Bring to mind a specific moment when your wound was active inside a relationship — not the whole relationship, one specific moment.

Scan your body from head to toe. Where do you feel the wound? Chest tightening, stomach dropping, throat closing, shoulders rising?

Now name what the wound made you do in that moment. Did you go quiet? Chase? Perform? Shrink? Over-explain?

Do not judge the behaviour. Just see it clearly. Say: “This is what the wound looks like in motion. I can see it now. Seeing it is the first step to interrupting it.”

7
The red flag you kept ignoring
What is the signal you have seen in more than one relationship and stayed anyway? What did you tell yourself each time to justify it?
♡ Recognition meditation

Close your eyes. Bring to mind the moment you first saw the red flag you named. The actual moment — not the memory you have polished, the raw one.

Notice what you felt in your body then. Notice what you did instead of acting on it.

Say to yourself: “I saw it. I chose to look away. I understand now why I did. And I am building a different response for next time.”

This is not self-punishment. It is pattern-closing.

C — Your patterns

Patterns are the wound in motion. They run without your permission unless you make them visible.

8
The thread across relationships
Look at more than one relationship. What is the common thread? Not the men — the feeling they activated. What kept coming back?
♡ Thread meditation

Sit quietly. Bring to mind two or three of the significant relationships you have had.

Do not think about the men themselves. Think about the feeling that showed up in each one. The familiar feeling. The one you kept coming back to.

Ask yourself: “What is this feeling reminding me of? When did I first feel it?”

Stay with the question for 5 minutes. You do not need an answer immediately — you need the question to land.

9
What you confuse with love
What feeling do you mistake for love or connection? Intensity, being needed, anxiety, the chase, being rescued, feeling special to someone unavailable?
♡ Distinction meditation

Bring to mind the last time you felt what you described — the intensity, the anxiety, the pull, the feeling of being chosen by someone unavailable.

Locate it in your body. Where do you feel it? Chest, stomach, throat?

Now bring to mind a moment of genuine calm connection — with a friend, a family member, anyone who made you feel safe without drama. Locate that in your body.

Feel the difference. That difference is the compass. The thing you confuse with love lives in your body as urgency. Real love lives as ease.

10
How safe you have been as a partner
Honestly — how safe have you been for a partner to be vulnerable with? Have you been consistent, regulated, non-retaliatory? Where have you caused harm?
♡ Accountability meditation

This one requires courage. Sit quietly and bring to mind a partner you were not fully safe for.

Not to punish yourself — to see clearly. What did your behaviour look like when you were reactive, inconsistent, or withdrawn? What impact did it have?

Say: “I caused harm here. I understand now where it came from. And I am choosing to become someone safer.”

Breathe. The capacity to hold your own accountability without collapsing or defending is one of the most mature things a person can do.

D — Your navigation plan

Knowing the wound is not enough. You need a specific plan for what to do when it fires inside a new relationship — before your feelings negotiate on its behalf.

11
Your activation triggers
What specific things does a man do or not do that activate your wound? What is the earliest signal — before it becomes a full emotional response?
♡ Trigger awareness meditation

Sit still. Bring to mind a recent moment when your wound activated inside a relationship — the moment just before the emotional response, not the response itself.

What was happening in the room? What did he do or not do? What did your body feel first?

Stay with that earliest signal — the tightening, the shift, the thing that happened before the reaction. That is the trigger.

Say: “I know what this feels like now. And next time I feel it, I will pause before I respond.”

12
Your response plan
When the wound activates in a new relationship, what is your specific plan? Write it as a sequence — not a vague intention.
♡ Rehearsal meditation

Read back the response plan you just wrote.

Now close your eyes and rehearse it. Imagine your wound activating in a new relationship — feel the trigger signal in your body — and then watch yourself follow the plan. Step by step. Slowly.

The brain does not distinguish between a vividly imagined experience and a real one. Rehearsing the plan in your body makes it available when you need it.

Do this once now. Then read the plan again before you begin Phase 1.

13
The non-negotiable you will not move on
Based on everything you know about your wound and your pattern, what is the one thing you will absolutely not do again? Name it and explain why.
♡ Commitment meditation

Read the non-negotiable you wrote out loud. Even quietly. Even to yourself.

Feel whether you believe it. Not whether you want to believe it — whether you actually do.

If doubt rises, do not suppress it. Ask it: “What are you protecting?” The answer is usually more wound. Write it in the notes field if it comes up.

If you believe it, say: “This is not negotiable. Not for comfort, not for loneliness, not for love.”

E — Your readiness

Not readiness for him. Readiness to observe clearly — with your own life full and your own needs met, so you are not looking for someone to complete something in you.

14
What healed looks like
Describe the version of you with this wound genuinely healed, inside a relationship. What does she do differently? What does she no longer tolerate? What does she feel?
♡ Future self meditation

Close your eyes. Imagine the version of you who has done this work — the woman with this wound genuinely healed. Not perfect. Healed.

Watch her in a moment of conflict inside a relationship. Watch how she holds her ground without cruelty, how she states her needs without apology, how she leaves what does not serve her without drama.

Feel what it feels like to be her — in your chest, your stomach, your posture.

That feeling is not a fantasy. It is a direction. Stay in it for 5 minutes.

15
Your life, regardless of him
One year from now, single and whole — what does your good life look like? One specific scene. Not a partnered life. Yours.
♡ Future self meditation

Close your eyes. See yourself one year from now — no one else, just you. Laughing with friends. Doing work that matters. Sleeping peacefully. Walking somewhere with no one to answer to. Feel the satisfaction in your body. Ask: “What is one small step toward this vision this week?”

16
What you are building while you observe
Phase 1 asks you to watch without emotional investment. What are you building for yourself during that time? What will your life look like while you are watching?
♡ Anchor meditation

Bring to mind the things you named that you are building. Your work, your friendships, your health, your healing.

Feel them as real — not abstract. Where in your body do you feel a sense of purpose and investment when you think about them?

Say: “My life does not wait for him. My life is happening now. He is being observed from inside a full life, not from an empty one.”

That fullness is your greatest protection in Phase 1. A woman with a full life observes clearly. A woman waiting for her life to begin does not.
