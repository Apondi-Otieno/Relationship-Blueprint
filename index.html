A — Closing the last chapter

Before a new chapter begins, the last one must close. These three questions are about the most significant relationship — or the one that still lives rent-free in your head.

A1
Your honest 50%
What was your part in how it ended? Not his failures — yours specifically. What did you do, avoid, permit, or ignore that you already knew?
♡ Freedom meditation

Sit quietly, one hand on your heart. Breathe in: “Something happened, and I played a part. I am not a victim and I am not a villain. I am someone who is learning to see clearly.”

On each exhale, release one specific thing you did that you wish you had done differently — not with shame, with honesty.

A2
What you are still waiting for
What are you still waiting for him to give you? An apology, closure, an explanation, proof that you mattered? Name it — then name how you could begin giving it to yourself.
♡ Release meditation

Visualise a cord connecting your belly to theirs. With a slow out-breath, dissolve it. Say: “I release you to your path. I reclaim my energy for my own life.” Repeat 3 times.

A3
The gift
What did this relationship genuinely give you — even if it hurt? A lesson, a boundary you will now hold, a red flag you will never ignore again.
♡ Alchemy meditation

Think of the smallest genuinely kind thing he did. Breathe in: “May you be happy.” If anger rises: “May you find a parking space.” This is not for him. It is to unclench your heart.

B — Your wounds

A wound is not a flaw. It is a place that was hurt before you had the tools to protect it. Unnamed wounds make all your relationship decisions on your behalf.

B4
Name the wound
Which of these do you recognise? Tap to add, then write about it:

Fear of abandonment I am not enough I must earn love Fear of being controlled Fear of intimacy I am invisible I am too much I self-abandon to keep peace I do not trust my own judgment I am only valuable if useful
B5
Where it came from
When did you first feel this? A parent, an early experience of loss, humiliation, or neglect? You do not need to over-explain — just locate it.
♡ Inner child meditation

Imagine your wise, current self meeting your inner child at the age you first felt this wound. Hold her hand. Say: “I see you. You are safe now. I will never leave you.” Stay 5–10 minutes.

B6
How it behaves inside a relationship
When this wound is active, what does it make you do? What do you tolerate, chase, avoid, perform, or become?
♡ Body scan meditation

Bring to mind one specific moment when your wound was active. Scan your body — where do you feel it? Chest, stomach, throat? Now name what the wound made you do. Say: “This is what the wound looks like in motion. Seeing it is the first step to interrupting it.”

B7
The red flag you kept ignoring
What is the signal you have seen in more than one relationship and stayed anyway? What did you tell yourself each time to justify it?

C — Your patterns

Patterns are the wound in motion. They run without your permission unless you make them visible.

C8
The thread across relationships
Look at more than one relationship. What is the common thread? Not the men — the feeling they activated. What kept coming back?
C9
What you confuse with love
What feeling do you mistake for love or connection? Intensity, being needed, anxiety, the chase, being rescued, feeling special to someone unavailable?
C10
How safe you have been as a partner
Honestly — how safe have you been for a partner to be vulnerable with? Where have you been inconsistent, reactive, or caused harm?
0/3 answered
D — Your navigation plan

Knowing the wound is not enough. You need a specific plan for what to do when it fires inside a new relationship — before your feelings negotiate on its behalf.

D11
Your activation triggers
What specific things does a man do or not do that activate your wound? What is the earliest signal — before it becomes a full emotional response?
D12
Your response plan
When the wound activates in a new relationship, what is your specific plan? Write it as a sequence — not a vague intention.
D13
The non-negotiable you will not move on
Based on everything you know about your wound and your pattern, what is the one thing you will absolutely not do again?
0/3 answered
E — Your readiness

Not readiness for him. Readiness to observe clearly — with your own life full, so you are not looking for someone to complete something in you.

E14
What healed looks like
Describe the version of you with this wound genuinely healed, inside a relationship. What does she do differently? What does she no longer tolerate?
E15
Your good life without anyone
One year from now, single and whole — what does your good life look like? Not a partnered life. Yours. One specific scene.
♡ Future self meditation

Close your eyes. See yourself one year from now — no one else, just you. Laughing with friends. Doing work that matters. Sleeping peacefully. Ask: “What is one small step toward this vision this week?”

E16
What you are building while you observe
Phase 1 asks you to watch without emotional investment. What are you building for yourself during that time?

Dating Readiness Check

Before you step in.

Five real scenarios. Answer honestly — not how you wish you would respond, but how you actually would right now.

Scenario 1 of 5
You’ve been on 3 dates. He texts to reschedule last minute — for the second time. What do you do?
Scenario 2 of 5
Three weeks in, he still hasn’t made plans more than 48 hours in advance. Your gut says something is off. What do you do?
Scenario 3 of 5
A friend asks how things are going. Honestly, you feel more anxious than excited. What does that mean to you?
Scenario 4 of 5
He says something dismissive when you try to share something vulnerable. What do you do?
Scenario 5 of 5
Two months in, he still hasn’t introduced you to anyone in his life. Do you bring it up?
